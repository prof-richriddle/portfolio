How much pressure can a system of polity absorb before it breaks or strikes back? With Star Trek’s Borg Collective as a frame, this essay reads five films (<i>The Passion of Joan of Arc</i>, <i>Day of Wrath</i>, <i>The Prisoner</i>, <i>Inherit the Wind</i>, and <i>Elmer Gantry</i>) alongside real-world pressures on religion, sexuality, and journalism: the policing of women’s dress, present-day witchcraft accusations in Nigeria, churches that rely on gay men’s talents while rejecting their queer identity, and citizen journalism in 2020, including an interview with a photojournalism scholar. It concludes that if perfect harmony means giving up what sets a person apart, it is not worth the cost.
