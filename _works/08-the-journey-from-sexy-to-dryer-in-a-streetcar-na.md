The 1951 film of <i>A Streetcar Named Desire</i> cut three defining moments of sexual expression from Tennessee Williams’s play: Stella’s return to Stanley, Blanche’s seduction of a young paperboy, and Stanley’s assault on Blanche. Comparing the play, the 1951 release, and the restored 1993 version, I argue that these cuts strip the characters of their motivations and hide the true source of trouble at 632 Elysian Fields: desire.
