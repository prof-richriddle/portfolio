Tony Gilroy’s <i>Michael Clayton</i> (2007) follows three attorneys through a class action lawsuit in which the defendant really is guilty. Adapting law professor Monroe Freedman’s “trilemma,” I weigh each attorney’s choices against three duties: to the client, to the public trust, and to their own conscience. I argue that the party most responsible is not any one attorney but the law firm of Kenner, Bach and Ledeen, the corrupt system behind them.
