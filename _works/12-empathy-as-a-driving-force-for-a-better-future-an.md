<i>X-Men: Days of Future Past</i> (2014) asks viewers to empathize with mutants, a species that is entirely fictional, and in doing so it retcons a struggling film franchise. Drawing on Keen’s <i>Empathy and the Novel</i> and Stadler’s work on empathy in film, I close-read three moments, ending with the conversation between the older and younger Xavier, which I argue is the film’s most empathetic. Mutants can stand in for the Other, telling those stories without the confrontation of direct political speech.
