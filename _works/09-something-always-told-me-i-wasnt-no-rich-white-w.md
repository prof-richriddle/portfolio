I compare the 1961 film of <i>A Raisin in the Sun</i> with Hansberry’s unfilmed original screenplay, published in 1992, and argue that studio “white fear” shaped both what was filmed and how it was shot. Through those cuts and choices, a raw call for equality became an oversimplified “happy ending” meant to ease white guilt. Using critical media literacy, I look at how the film represents its Black characters, a studio memo addressing that fear, and race as the deciding factor in where the family can live.
