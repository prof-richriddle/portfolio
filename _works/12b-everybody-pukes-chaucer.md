Like <i>The Canterbury Tales</i>, this essay comes with a prologue and a retraction. It reads the Wife of Bath and the Pardoner for how Chaucer represents gender and sexual Otherness, and for how religious identity limited what he could write. The Wife of Bath’s tale seems to hand a wife power, then undercuts it, and the Pardoner’s queerness stays veiled in allusion. I argue that the patriarchal, queer-phobic views that constrained Chaucer still hold in secular and religious institutions today, from a present-day church leader’s writings to the protest signs outside American churches. As the title, borrowed from a <i>Mad TV</i> sketch, puts it: everybody pukes.
