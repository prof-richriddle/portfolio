Of the roughly 1.5 billion people who speak English, more than a billion know another language; in the US, only about 1 in 5 people do, and most internet users are not native English speakers. This presentation questions the usual reasons for demanding English-only technical communication, from air traffic control and trade to software, education, and development. Drawing on Li, de Jong, and Karreman’s call for more research on technical translation, and on Gonzales’s call for a justice-driven reframing of translators’ often invisible work, it asks: who are we trusting to keep us connected to the rest of the world?
