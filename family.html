Friends & Family, Start Here!

As a doctoral student, I move through three stages: coursework, qualifying exams, and the dissertation. At any of those stages, what I’m studying can become a conference paper (a presentation), and a conference paper can become an article or a chapter in a book. There’s no single path from one to the next. Here’s how one project made the trip.

  1. A class proposal

    Fall 2025: a semester project proposal on the Thai series Khemjira as a site for studying Thai rhetoric, written for a history of rhetoric seminar.

  2. A conference talk

    March 2026: “Khemjira Must Survive: Queer Longing and Thai Spirituality at the Intersection of Thai Rhetoric,” at the Gods and Monsters Conference.

  3. A book chapter

    Forthcoming: “Khemjira: The Queering of Thai Lakorn’s Cosmology,” in a collected edition from the conference (McFarland).

What each stage means

Coursework

Each class meets once a week for 15 weeks. Every week I do the reading for class and write things like reading responses, project proposals, and presentations, ending with a final paper.

Qualifying exams

My committee and I build a reading list of somewhere between about 100 books and 400 articles. Then, over four days, I write answers to some very hard questions about it.

Dissertation

I do my own research, write it up as a book-length study, and then defend it in front of my committee.
