Read Me

How this site works

Home points you to three places. About Me is who I am and how I teach, with a few of my class activities. Friends & Family explains how academic work grows, from a class paper to a book chapter. The Portfolio holds the work itself: sort it by type, filter it by topic, and open any tile to read more. Class papers appear as short summaries under Coursework; three pieces appear in full as writing samples.

Why GitHub Pages

I built this site on GitHub Pages using Jekyll, GitHub’s built-in publishing system, instead of a website builder like Wix or Google Sites. Three reasons:

  1. Each piece of work is its own small text file. To fix a typo or add a paper, I edit one file, and the site rebuilds itself.
  2. The design is kept separate from the content, so changing the look never touches the writing.
  3. It’s free, there are no ads or platform branding, and I own every file.

Designing for AI

I chose AI-resilient over AI-ready, and I applied it to the Coursework section.

AI-ready design makes a site easier for AI tools to find and index. That works against choices I made on purpose: I keep some of my research difficult for bad actors to immediately find and leverage to misrepresent my research and teaching philosophy.

AI-resilient design protects voice, intent, and context from “flattening.” In Coursework, that risk was concrete. Four of my titles quote someone else, and one quotes a professor’s classroom remark that my paper critiques. On this page, quotation marks and a summary show whose words they are. In an AI summary, a title can travel alone, and quotation marks are the first thing to go.

So every title that quotes someone else now has a line directly under it naming the speaker. That line sits with the title, not inside the summary you have to open, so the title and its source get copied together.
