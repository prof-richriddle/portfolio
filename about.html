About Me

Life & Teaching

I was a bit aimless as a college student until I found my love for English. Then I found rhetoric and composition, and everything changed. Forever.

I teach writing & rhetoric and technical communication, and I’m a doctoral student at Texas Tech University, pursuing a Ph.D. in Technical Communication and Rhetoric. I hold an M.A. in Rhetoric and Composition from Texas State University.

My interests vary from Thai rhetoric and TV to queerness at large, and from creative pedagogies that bring students’ attention back into the process, to what AI does to language and meaning.

How I Work

I teach university courses on writing & rhetoric, and technical communication. In my classes, students learn to give opposing arguments a fair hearing; they defend the side they disagree with as well as the one they first connected to. They also learn to break an argument into parts: the claim, the evidence for it, and the warrant (the often unstated assumption that links the two). Classic texts stay on the table, and so does the right to question them. Our families, communities, and society at large shape what we accept as true, so what feels like common sense often rests on assumptions our communities have taught us to take for granted. My job is to keep asking “How do you know?” no matter which side a claim comes from.

I don’t pressure students into using AI or LLMs (large language models), nor do I forbid them. The goal of a writing course is still that students learn the process of writing, and that goal has not changed. At the same time, the tools available to writers are changing, and I do not pretend the question of how to preserve learning alongside them is settled. What I can do is keep the process visible: students write in stages, share drafts in peer review, and, when they use AI, record their prompts and what they kept or changed.

Classes I Teach

Writing & rhetoric

Exploring ideas through critical reading, purposeful writing, and revision, with attention to audience, context, and purpose.

Research & argument

Developing persuasive arguments through inquiry, careful evaluation of sources, and responsible use of evidence.

Technical communication

Creating clear, usable documents for professional audiences, with an emphasis on ethical communication and effective design.

Writing for international students

Helping international students navigate the expectations of college writing and develop their academic voices.

Teaching formats

In person, synchronous online, and asynchronous online.
